Why voice typing

You think faster than you type.

Most people speak at 130–160 words a minute and type at 40. Dictation closes that gap without moving your hands off the mouse, and without sending a single second of audio to a server.

3–4× faster than typing

Write the email, the ticket, the note, the commit message at the speed you talk. Cleanup fixes numbers, punctuation and “um” before it lands.

~1–1.5× realtime on CPU

Your hands never leave the mouse

One side button starts and stops it. Nothing to click, no window to switch to, no overlay to dismiss first — just talk into the field you are already in.

XButton2, or XButton1

A side button, the middle click, F8 or Ctrl+Alt+J — press it once and the app records it as your trigger. No editing files, no restart, no dialog to fight with.

Auto clipboard + history

Your last five copies, one click away.

Dictation is only half of typing. The other half is moving text around: a link from a ticket, an address from a chat, a query you want to run again.

This app keeps the last few things that passed through the clipboard. Left-click the traffic light and they appear in a small panel; click one and it goes straight back on the clipboard, ready to paste anywhere.

How it works

One button, no workflow to learn.

Press, talk, release. The rest — language detection, cleanup, pasting, clipboard — happens on its own.

Hold your shortcut

Out of the box that is the mouse side button. Or press any key, combination or click once in the recorder and it becomes the trigger — no restart.

Speak normally

Qwen3-ASR 1.7B transcribes 16 kHz audio locally. Pin a language for short clips, or leave it on Auto for mixed speech.

Release

Silence is trimmed, numbers and punctuation are tidied, the text is pasted where your cursor was and stays on the clipboard.

shortcut hook → start_recording() → 16 kHz frames → release → trim + guards → Qwen3-ASR → cleanup → Ctrl+V / Cmd+V → clipboard history

Questions

The things people ask first.

Does it send my audio or text anywhere?

No. Recognition runs on your CPU through a local copy of Qwen3-ASR, and the app forces HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 so even library update checks cannot phone home. The only network use is the one-time model download you start yourself.

What does “auto clipboard” change?

Normally a paste helper restores whatever was on your clipboard before. Here the transcript stays on the clipboard instead, so you can paste it into a second app without dictating again. Set "auto_clipboard": false to restore the old behaviour.

How does the clipboard history work?

Every text that lands on the clipboard is recorded (newest first, de-duplicated, five items by default — change clipboard_history_size). Left-click the traffic light and the list appears; click an item to copy it back. The panel auto-hides and never takes focus.

Will it work in an app running as administrator?

Windows blocks synthetic keystrokes from a normal process into an elevated one. Use tray → Restart as Administrator for those.

Can I choose my own shortcut?

Yes. Tray → Shortcut → Detect a new shortcut…, then press a side button, the middle click, F8 or a combination such as Ctrl+Alt+J. It is saved and applied immediately. A bare letter is refused on purpose so normal typing is never hijacked.

Does it break the browser back/forward buttons?

Not on Windows: the low-level mouse hook swallows the side button before the browser sees it, so you dictate instead of navigating. macOS and Linux can only observe the shortcut, so pick a combination those apps do not already use.

Can it handle more than English?

Yes — Auto detects the language, and you can pin English, Chinese, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, French, German or Russian from the tray. Pinning stops short clips from flipping language.

What about a Mac or Linux?

Both are supported from the same folder: setup.sh and run_dictate.sh for macOS/Linux, with Cmd+V, a non-activating NSPanel on macOS and xclip/wl-clipboard on Linux. Linux needs an X11 session — Wayland blocks global hooks and tray icons. Windows is the platform that has been tested on real hardware so far.
